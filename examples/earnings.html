What drove the result

  • Management View · Management attributed Q4 growth to very strong DRAM and NAND pricing alongside higher bit shipments: DRAM revenue was $39.8 billion (+343% year over year), with bits up mid-single digits sequentially and prices up in the high teens; NAND was $14.1 billion (+526% year over year), with bits up about 10% and prices up about 30% sequentially. [8] [6 · p. 23, 24] [7 · p. 4]
  • Fact · All four business units set Q4 revenue records. Core Data Center led sequential growth (+56%) on higher pricing and shipments; Cloud Memory grew 18% sequentially on the same factors. Management said Mobile and Client growth was partly offset by lower shipments as product was allocated to other businesses. [5] [6 · p. 24, 25] [8]
  • Management View · Micron said 26 multi-year take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements represented an estimated more than 35% of revenue through 2030, with customer financial commitments of $32 billion. The agreements improve demand visibility, but a majority of the defined pricing bands have undisclosed floors and ceilings. [7 · p. 2] [8] [10]
  • Management View · Management said fiscal Q1 gross margin should be the low point for FY2027 because higher FY2026 incentive compensation was absorbed into Q4 inventory and will flow through Q1 cost of sales; it expects higher margins for the rest of FY2027, with a more moderate pace of price increases. [7 · p. 9] [8] [6 · p. 30]

Risks and watch points

  • Memory pricing and margins are cyclical. Micron's 10-K lists average-selling-price volatility, customer and end-market dependence, competition, geopolitics, AI uncertainty, and realizing returns on capacity expansions among its risks; it says DRAM and NAND annual ASP changes ranged from about +200% to -50% over the prior five fiscal years. [2 · p. 23]
  • The planned capacity build requires large, long-lead investments. Micron warns of construction delays, cost overruns, slow or uneconomic production ramps, reduced government incentives, and the risk that demand may not use added capacity. [2 · p. 29] [7 · p. 9]
  • The company itself expects possible double-digit unit declines in PC and smartphone markets despite higher premium-product revenue; it also said server memory-content growth is tracking somewhat below earlier expectations. [6 · p. 14] [8] [10]
  • The beat-and-raise result received a muted immediate share response, while analysts highlighted that stock expectations were already high and that peak-cycle earnings can make valuation multiples look deceptively low. [9] [10] [12]

Management and analyst perspectives

  • Analyst coverage broadly agreed that AI demand, price increases, and scarce memory supply supported near-term revenue and margins. TheStreet reported that Rosenblatt analyst Kevin Cassidy maintained Buy and raised his target to $1,900 from $1,500; StockTitan noted Q1 company revenue guidance of $61.5 billion was above its cited LSEG consensus of $57 billion. [9] [10] [11]
  • The central difference is confidence in durability. Management argues multi-year take-or-pay agreements and AI-driven shortages make FY2027 stronger and supply tighter through calendar 2028. StockTitan cautions that past memory booms saw abrupt margin collapses and that today's low forward P/E may reflect peak-cycle earnings; TheStreet says demand weakening earlier than expected would put pricing and cash-return plans at risk. [7 · p. 2, 5] [8] [10] [9]
  • Sell-side questions on the call focused on the absence of a full-year FY2027 capex figure, how much cash Micron would return, and whether customers might reduce memory specifications as prices rise. Management reaffirmed its supply-tightness view and said second-half capex would exceed first-half spending, but did not provide a full-year capex or cash-return target. [8] [7 · p. 10] [10]

What drove the result

  • Fact · Q4 revenue was $18.68B (+6% USD, +7% local currency), above the company’s $17.75B–$18.40B revenue guide; all three geographic markets and all five industry groups grew in local currency. [14 · p. 2]
  • Management View · Management attributed demand to large-scale reinventions, ecosystem work and data/AI embedded across engagements; management said revenue from emerging data and AI players doubled and bookings tripled for the year. [20 · p. 13]
  • Management View · Management said Q4 upside had several contributors: more small deals, faster contract mobilization, some acquisition and federal outperformance, and PTO carryover that increased available staffing; the CFO said no single factor was material. [20 · p. 13]
  • Fact · Managed Services grew faster than Consulting in FY26: $37.30B revenue and +6% local-currency growth versus $36.88B and +3%; management linked Managed Services growth to Technology Managed Services and Operations. [14 · p. 4, 10] [20 · p. 4, 10]
  • Fact · FY26 GAAP operating margin expanded to 15.4% from 14.7%; the non-GAAP adjusted margin was 15.8%, excluding $307.5M of FY26 business-optimization costs. FY26 GAAP diluted EPS was $13.56 and adjusted diluted EPS was $13.97. [14 · p. 5, 6, 13]
  • Management View · Management attributed FY27 growth guidance to 2%–2.5% inorganic contribution and described an approximately $5B acquisition investment plan; that contribution leaves the remainder of the 3%–6% range to organic growth and FX arithmetic. [20 · p. 11]
  • Management View · The FY27 guide allows the top end to assume no growth in discretionary spending and the bottom end to allow deterioration; management said the roughly $1B annualized direct Middle East headwind is expected to continue and is included in guidance. [20 · p. 15, 17]
  • Fact · Q4 cash from operations was $3.10B versus $3.91B a year earlier, while days sales outstanding rose to 50 days from 47; FY26 property and equipment additions were $0.74B and free cash flow was $11.62B. [14 · p. 4, 6]
  • Analyst Opinion · Analyst commentary sees AI transformation and bookings demand broadening, but treats low-single-digit organic growth, pricing, and uncertain sustainability as the main open questions. [21 · p. ] [22 · p. ] [23 · p. ]

Risks and watch points

  • FY27 reported revenue growth includes management’s expected 2%–2.5% inorganic contribution; analyst commentary calculates that the 3%–6% overall guide leaves a broad roughly 0.5%–4% organic range, rather than establishing accelerating organic growth. [14 · p. 7, 11] [20 · p. 7, 11] [23 · p. 7, 11]
  • Management said the Middle East direct headwind worsened in Q4 and was about $1B annualized; it expects the conditions to continue, while the bottom of guidance allows for further deterioration. [20 · p. 13, 17]
  • Management reported lower pricing in many areas during Q4 and described FY27 as remaining intensely competitive; AI-enabled productivity may pressure like-for-like pricing even as the company seeks to sell broader work. [20 · p. 14, 18]
  • Discretionary client spending was described by management as stable but weak; management’s FY27 range allows for deterioration at the low end. [21 · p. 15] [20 · p. 15]
  • DSO increased to 50 days from 47 days year over year and Q4 operating cash flow declined to $3.10B from $3.91B; these are cash-conversion items to monitor alongside elevated acquisitions. [14 · p. 4, 10] [20 · p. 4, 10]
  • The Q3 FY26 10-Q said there were no material changes to risk factors disclosed in the FY25 10-K; the full FY26 10-K was not available by the as-of date. [16 · p. ] [18 · p. ]

What drove the result

  • Fact · Q4 GAAP revenue increased 6.3% year over year to USD 634.7 million; organic revenue increased 7.1%. Across FY2026, GAAP and organic revenue each grew 6.7% on their respective bases. [25 · p. 1]
  • Management View · Management attributed Q4 GAAP margin compression to higher employee compensation, including restructuring charges and ASV-linked incentive plans. The adjusted margin decline was attributed mainly to ASV-linked incentive compensation and technology expense. [25 · p. 1]
  • Fact · For FY2026, GAAP operating margin declined to 28.3% from 32.2%, while adjusted margin declined to 34.5% from 36.3%. Management cited restructuring, ASV-linked incentives and one-time CEO compensation for GAAP costs; it cited incentive compensation and technology expenses for the adjusted decline. [25 · p. 1]
  • Management View · GAAP diluted EPS fell 15.4% in Q4 and 6.3% for FY2026. The company attributed the decline to higher operating expenses (including non-recurring costs) and the absence of the prior-year divestiture gain, partly offset by revenue growth and a lower share count. Adjusted diluted EPS grew 11.6% in Q4 and 6.1% for the year, with revenue growth and fewer shares cited as drivers. [25 · p. 1]
  • Management View · Management described revenue growth and client retention as resilient, citing renewal rates above 95%, longer renewal terms, and growth in AI-related ASV. It said AI offers contributed more than 10% of Q4 ASV flow and that it was investing in data readiness, cloud delivery and cyber resilience. [29]
  • Management View · Management said FY2026 margins were a low point and forecast a 25–75 basis-point improvement in FY2027, while cautioning that the year would remain second-half weighted. It rejected the idea that the FY2026 ASV result reflected pull-forward demand. [29]
  • Inference · The full-year segment revenue approximations indicate the Americas remained the largest region; Asia Pacific had the fastest approximate reported-revenue growth. These figures are derived from exact 9M amounts plus Q4 amounts rounded to USD 0.1 million, so small rounding differences are possible. [26] [25]

Risks and watch points

  • Growth may slow: organic ASV increased 7.0% in FY2026, while management guided to 5.0%–6.5% for FY2027. This is company guidance, not a comparison with analyst consensus. [25 · p. 1]
  • Margins remain under pressure from incentive compensation, restructuring and technology/cloud investment. FY2027 guidance implies a modest adjusted-margin recovery to 34.75%–35.25% from FY2026's 34.5%, but remains below FY2025's 36.3%; execution is required. [25 · p. 1] [29 · p. 1]
  • Analysts raised competition and monetization questions as data and workflow are distributed through AI products and protocols. Management argued that its combination of data, analytics and workflow plus cloud and protocol integrations supports its position; whether new AI use converts into durable revenue remains an execution risk. [29] [31]
  • The FY2025 Form 10-K identifies risks from competition, third-party data/content suppliers and cybersecurity; the Q3 FY2026 10-Q refers readers back to the annual filing for risk factors. [27] [26]
  • Management noted FY2027 interest expense from the 2.9% notes due in March 2027 and a higher tax rate as EPS headwinds; refinancing and tax outcomes could affect the forecast. [29]

Open selection criteria, accounting notes and period caveats

Selection

The main agent searched recent public technology and technology-information company results, checked publication dates on company Investor Relations pages, and selected the newest final quarterly or annual earnings reports available by the as-of timestamp. Upcoming earnings dates, preliminary results, and guidance-only releases were excluded. A tie among September 30 releases was resolved by selecting substantial public technology/data businesses.

  • Jabil (JBL) (2026-09-30): Its own results announcement labels FY26 results preliminary and unaudited; excluded under the instruction to omit preliminary releases. source
  • Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) (2026-10-15): The Q3 2026 results event was scheduled after the as-of timestamp, so it was an upcoming earnings date. source

Accounting and comparison bases

  • Actuals And Forecasts: Actual results are separated from management forecasts. Quarterly Q4 and full fiscal-year figures are kept in separate metric objects. Figures are in the company reporting currency; all selected companies report in U.S. dollars.
  • Revenue Growth: The comparison chart uses reported GAAP revenue growth in U.S. dollars. Accenture also reports local-currency growth; that is kept separately and excluded from the common chart. Micron FY2026 had 53 weeks versus 52 in FY2025; its reported growth is not week-normalized.
  • Gaap And Adjusted: GAAP diluted EPS and GAAP operating margin are shown in the main comparison. Non-GAAP adjusted values are labeled and reported separately in the detailed sections. FactSet organic ASV and organic revenue growth are non-GAAP operating measures, not GAAP revenue.
  • Segments: Company segment labels are preserved. Micron reports business-unit results; Accenture and FactSet provide geographic segment results, alongside additional revenue breakdowns. Revenue categories and reportable segments are not treated as interchangeable.
  • Capital Expenditure: Capex definitions differ and are not directly comparable: Micron reports net investment in capex after government incentives and PP&E sales; Accenture reports cash additions to property and equipment; FactSet includes cash purchases of property, equipment, leasehold improvements and capitalized internal-use software. FactSet standalone Q4 capex is derived from annual less nine-month cash purchases.
  • Consensus: A beat or miss is shown only where a dated source gives a same-period, comparable GAAP or adjusted EPS basis. Consensus is not a company result or forecast. Estimate snapshots can vary by source; discrepancies are retained as caveats.
  • Rounding: Amounts and growth rates use the precision and basis disclosed in the release or filing. Independently calculated revenue growth and derived segment/capex amounts are identified. Totals can differ slightly from rounded geographic or business-unit rows.
  • The latest fiscal quarters do not end on the same date. Accenture and FactSet Q4 FY2026 ended August 31, 2026; Micron Q4 FY2026 ended September 3, 2026.
  • Micron FY2026 contained 53 weeks, compared with 52 weeks in FY2025. No week-normalized revenue comparison was found.
  • All top-level comparison revenues and EPS are U.S. dollars. Revenue growth uses reported U.S. dollar results; Accenture’s local-currency growth is disclosed separately.
  • Capital expenditure is a company-defined or derived cash measure as described in Methods; dollar totals should not be read as directly comparable capital intensity.
  • FactSet FY2026 results were unaudited earnings-release figures because its FY2026 Form 10-K was not filed by the cutoff. Accenture FY2026 10-K was also not available by the cutoff; its Q4/FY earnings release supplies the latest results. Micron FY2026 Form 10-K was filed October 9, 2026.
